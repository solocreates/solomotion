# 3D worlds

A **world** is a comp whose layers are a 3D scene. Its picture is what its live camera sees; nest it into a flat comp and it is one layer there, like any precomp. Everything in it is an ordinary layer — keys, easing, expressions, modulators, in/out animations, the timeline — so you already know how to animate it.

## Space

- **Z is up** (Blender). X right, Y away from the front view. **1 unit = 1 px = 1 cm**: a 200-unit cube stands as tall as 200 px of text.
- A layer's `x`, `y`, `z` is its **origin**; `rotationX`, `rotationY`, `rotation` (= Z) are degrees, Blender's XYZ order; `scale`, `scaleY`, `scaleZ`.
- The three positions, three rotations and three scales each key as one property — set them together.
- A text, image, video, shape, path or nested comp in a world is a **picture plane** by default: it stands up facing the front (−Y), at its own pixel size, in its exact colours. Rotate it like any object.
- Every one of them can also be a real **body** — the same three things a mesh has: **thickness** (`world_extrude`), a **material** (`world_material`, `material.*`) and **modifiers** (`effects_add {domain: "geometry"}`). A photo can be a printed block that bends like a page; a logo, gold letters that twist; a video, a screen with depth.

## Depth without a world

Any layer in a FLAT comp takes `z` (px, + is away), `rotationX` and `rotationY` (degrees) — it is drawn in perspective from the comp's front camera, where depth 0 is exactly 1:1. Use it for a card that leans, a title that lies back, a parallax push (`z` keyed), a flip (`rotationY` 0 → 180). Layers keep their stacking order; when depth should decide who is in front, or you want a camera move, move them into a world:

```
world_move_into {ids, name?}   → {nestedId, frameId}   same picture, now a world nested in their place
```

The world opens with a **Camera** layer that frames it exactly as before, so nothing moves; key its `x, y, z` and rotations to move through it. Inside, 2D `y` became world `−z` (up) and `z` became world `y` (depth) — keys included.

`world_move_into {frameId}` makes a WHOLE frame 3D: everything in it moves into a world nested full-frame inside it, and the frame itself stays (its place on the board, its size, its exports) as the wrapper. An empty frame gets a starter world. People get the same from the inspector's 3D button (beside Component), "+ Clip › 3D world", and ⌘P jumps to any world.

## Making a scene

```
world_create {name, width, height, starter?, transparent?}   → frameId (+ starter camera, light, studio, cube)
world_add {frameId, kind: mesh|camera|light|text, position:[x,y,z], lookAt?: [x,y,z] | layerId, …}
```

- `starter: true` (default) gives a framed cube, a key light and a studio — delete or restyle it; `starter: false` for a blank world.
- `transparent: true` when the world will be composited over 2D (a product floating over a layout).
- Meshes: `primitive` from `catalog_list primitives` (cube with `fillet` for rounded corners, uvSphere, icoSphere, cylinder, cone, pyramid, torus, capsule, tube, torusKnot, plane, grid, circle). `params` set its size (all keyable at `geometry.<param>`).
- Colour is the layer's **fill** (solid, gradient, image — `fill.<id>.color` keys as usual). The surface is `material`: `world_material {ids, preset}` (gold, chrome, glass, clay, ceramic, car paint, velvet, pearl, neon, toon, flat…) then fine-tune `material.roughness`, `material.metalness`, `material.transmission`, `material.emission`, `material.emissionStrength`.
- Put a world in a design: `layer_create {kind: "comp", sourceFrameId: <world>}` in the flat comp.

## Cameras

- The **live camera** is the topmost visible camera layer whose span covers the instant. Cut between angles by laying camera layers end to end on one lane (a camera track); stack lanes for alternates and hide the ones you are not using. No camera live = the default front camera.
- Aim with `lookAt` on `world_add`, or keep a camera on a subject with `world_track {id, lookAt: subjectId}` — it stays aimed while either moves. `follow` makes it ride along: its own position becomes its offset from the target.
- Lens: `camera.focalLength` (mm, 50 = natural; 24 wide; 85–135 portrait/product). Key it for a dolly-zoom.
- Camera moves are keys on `x, y, z` (and rotations, unless tracking). Ease them — `ease-in-out` for glides, a long `ease-out` to land.

## Controllers and rigs

`world_add {kind: "controller"}` adds an empty — axes that never render. Animate it and hang things off it: `world_track {id, childOf: parentId}` parents a layer (it stays where it is and follows from then on: moves, turns, scales). Chain them for a rig — hand → forearm → upper arm → controller — and key the joints' `rotation` for forward kinematics. A camera can be a child of a controller you orbit; `childOf: null` unparents.

**IK** — key a hand, not three rotations: `world_track {id: hand, ik: {target: goalId, chainLength: 2, pole?: elbowGoalId}}` turns the `chainLength` parents above the hand so it reaches the target (a controller you animate); `pole` picks which way the elbow bends. Anything else hung on the arm follows. Out of reach, the arm straightens toward the target. `ik: null` removes it.

## Physics

Physics is one system for worlds and flat comps — read `references/physics.md`. In a world: `physics_body {ids, type: "active"}` makes meshes AND 2D layers standing in it fall, collide and tumble; `type: "passive"` makes fixed colliders (a table, a ramp — rotate a flattened cube); `physics_force` stacks wind, vortices and pulls on nulls; rigged pieces swing from their bones (ball joints); `physics_joint` pins and springs; `physics_scene` sets gravity (981 = Earth, pulling down −Z), the ground (`floor`, at `floorAt`), `startMs`. Exact per frame — no baking. Tips: stack bodies with small gaps, give spheres `bounce` 0.5+, and aim a camera with `world_track lookAt` at a falling hero.

## Lights

`catalog_list lights`. Blender's units: point / spot / area in **watts** (`light.intensity` 500–3000 at a few metres), sun and environment in **strength** (sun 2–5, environment 0.3–1.5).

- A good default: an **environment** (`light: "environment"`, studio) at 0.3–0.6 for reflections and fill, plus one **sun** or **area** as the key, angled from the side.
- Rotate the environment (`rotation`) to move its highlights; key it for a turntable feel.
- Mist: the world's `fog` (frame inspector) for depth.

## Editing shapes

`mesh_edit {id, op, select}`: `extrude` (amount along the normal), `inset`, `subdivide`, `loopCut`, `merge`, `delete`, `fill`, `flip`, `recalc`, `move`/`rotate`/`scale` (the selected part), `mirror` (`axis`, across the selection's centre or `by`). People also cut with the knife (K) and draw strokes (Draw mode, D — see Drawing in a world); a path point's optional `z` (depth off its plane) makes the path a true 3D curve in a world — a ribbon of light round an object, a hand-drawn orbit. Select faces with `facing: "+z"` (the top), face indices, or `all`; select POINTS with `{mode: "vertex", verts}` or `side: "+z"` (the topmost points — a cylinder's rim, a lattice cage's top row). `op: "bridge"` joins two selected edge loops (or two face groups facing each other, which become a tunnel); `op: "spin"` sweeps the selection around `axis` through `by` by `deg` in `steps` — a profile into a vase. Add `proportional: {radius, falloff}` to a move/rotate/scale and the neighbours follow softly — a hill pulled from one vertex of a `grid`, a bulge on a face. A primitive becomes polygons on its first edit — its size params stop being keyable, so finish animating sizes first.

**Booleans**: group solids with a mode — `layers_group {ids, mode: "subtract"}` and the TOPMOST member cuts the rest (a hole, a notch, a scoop); `union` fuses them, `intersect` keeps the overlap. It stays live: move or animate the cutter and the cut follows. Cutters show as wire while working and never render.

MODIFIERS are cards on an object's own stack — a mesh's, or any 2D layer's in a world — the geometry domain of the effects verbs: `effects_add {id, domain: "geometry", type, values}` adds one (the result's `effectId` is the modifier id), `effects_update {id, effectId, values | enabled | index | remove}` edits, mutes, reorders or removes it, and `catalog_list modifiers` lists them with their fields. Order is meaning: mirror then subdivide is not subdivide then mirror. For non-destructive shape: subdivision (smooth), mirror, array (repeat), solidify (thickness), twist, bend, taper, wave and noise (they move on their own), smooth, spherify, **bevel** (`width`, `segments` 1 = chamfer / 3+ = round, `angle` limit — the single biggest step from CG-looking to real), and **particles** — the mesh becomes the particle: `mode` stream or burst, `count`, `lifetime`, `speed`, `spread`, `size`, `spin`, and force fields `gravity` (−600 default), `windX/windY`, `turbulence`; `showEmitter: true` keeps the source shape visible. Small low-poly shapes make the best particles (an icoSphere with subdivisions 1, a cube); emission colour makes sparks glow. Also **weld** (merge by distance), **decimate** (`ratio` — a low-poly facet look), **wireframe** (`thickness` struts on every edge, the blueprint look), **build** (`progress` 0 → 1 keyed: faces appear in `order` random / left to right / bottom to top / from the centre) and **explode** (`progress` 0 → 1 keyed: the surface shatters into its faces, flying `distance`, scattered `spread`, tumbling `spin`, one after another by `stagger`, falling by `gravity`). Exact per frame, so they scrub and export cleanly. Every field keys at `mdf.<modifierId>.<field>`.

Settings worth knowing: **mirror** takes any of X/Y/Z at once (with bisect and flip); **twist**, **bend** and **taper** take limits — act on only part of the shape (bend just the end of a word); **wave** has a falloff from its centre (a ripple from a drop); **array** is a `row` or a `circle` (`radius`, `arc` — a ring of copies round a point), each copy turned a step on X/Y/Z and scaled a step (a spiral staircase, a fanned deck).

Cards that READ ANOTHER OBJECT take its layer id in a field (it is a reference, never keyed): **curve** (`curve`: a path in the world — the shape rides it; key `offset` to slide text along a ribbon), **shrinkwrap** (`target`: a mesh — a label pressed onto a bottle, a logo onto a sphere; key `strength` 0 → 1 to press it on), **hook** (`target`: a controller — the part of the shape near where it stood follows its moves: pull, stretch, wobble), and **lattice** (below). **Scatter** spreads copies of another mesh (`source`, or small cubes) over the surface — stars on a planet, confetti on a title, rivets on a plate: `count` (key it and they appear one by one), `size` and `sizeRandom`, `turnRandom`, `offset` off the surface, `align` to stand along it, `keep` the surface or leave only the copies; on a picture's body each copy wears the picture's colour where it landed. Also **displace** (`source: "picture"` — a body pushed out by its own picture's brightness: an embossed logo, a relief from a photo — or `noise` for terrain), **screw** (a path's outline turned round an axis — a vase, a goblet, a lathe; `angle` 360, `height` for a spring), and **remesh** (`blocks` voxels or `smooth` — a melted, uniform surface).

**Lattice** — the soft squash-and-lean everyone wants: `world_lattice {id, resolution?: [u, v, w]}` puts a cage of points round the object (3×3×3 by default), riding it, with a Lattice card reading it; returns `{cageId, modifierId}`. Reshape the CAGE and the object follows: `mesh_edit {id: cageId, op: "move", select: {side: "+z"}, by: [80, 0, 0]}` leans the top; `op: "scale"` on `side: "-z"` pinches the base. Moving the whole cage changes nothing (only its shape does); the cage never renders. Key the card's `strength` 0 → 1 to press a squash in. It stays live — there is nothing to apply.

On a 2D layer the stack runs on its body — its card (flat), its slab or its outline solid — and the picture stays on the surface wherever the stack takes it. A bending stack gets points automatically: `bend` (`axis: "x"`) on a photo curls it like a page, `wave` ripples it like a flag, `twist` wrings a word. On a flat card these deform the picture itself; add thickness first for a solid.

`world_apply_modifier {id, modifierId, atMs?}` (meshes) bakes the stack up to and including that card into the mesh's polygons — the look does not change, those cards go, a primitive becomes editable. Apply when a shape is final and you want to edit its points.

## Sculpting

`world_sculpt {id, brush, strokes: [{points: [[x,y,z]…], radius?, strength?}], radius, strength, direction?, falloff?, symmetry?, detail?}` shapes a mesh's surface like Blender's sculpt mode — points are OBJECT space (as `mesh_edit`; read the extent from `world_inspect`), a stroke is dabbed every ~12% of the radius along the polyline. `draw` raises along the surface (`direction: -1` carves), `clay` builds flat layers, `inflate` swells, `crease` cuts a fold, `smooth` relaxes, `flatten` presses to the area's plane, `pinch` gathers, `grab` drags what is under the FIRST point by (last − first). A primitive has too few points to shape: pass `detail` (smooth subdivision, ×4 faces a level — a cube wants 3–4, a sphere 1–2). `symmetry: ["x"]` mirrors every dab. One undo step; check with `view_render` at "standard". In the app people sculpt in Sculpt mode (the header's mode switch): with something selected the brush touches only that, otherwise whatever is under it; every brush also shapes drawings (Draw lifts a line toward the view, Flatten presses its depth back to the page).

## Drawing in a world

A stroke drawn in the viewport (Draw mode) is an ordinary PATH layer standing on a page: facing the view through the 3D cursor by default, on surfaces (with its tube lifted off them by half its width plus an offset), or on the front/side/top plane. Strokes on one page join one layer, so its closed loops fill (Draw › Fill) and it moves as one. To make the same with the API, `layer_create {kind: "path"}` in the world and place it with `x/y/z` and `rotationX/rotation` — its content stands on its local XZ plane facing −Y; give points `z` for a 3D curve (a handle may carry its own absolute `z`; left out, it follows the line between the two points). A selected drawing is the page people keep drawing on from any angle, so a scene of several drawings — near, middle, far — is several path layers (or groups of them) at different depths, which gives parallax as the camera moves. In Edit mode people shape its points like the pen tool (smooth/corner, handles, merge, extrude, join).

## Thickness — solid text, logos, photo blocks

`world_extrude {ids, depth, bevel?, bevelSegments?, sideColor?, fit?}` gives ANY 2D layer in a world thickness — extruded letters, a logo, a coin, a photo printed on a block, a video slab. The face keeps the layer's own picture (every script; Farsi and Arabic join as they do flat), `bevel` 2–6 rounds the edges into highlights, depth runs away from the front so the face stays where the flat picture was.

- `fit: "outline"` (default for text, shapes, paths and images) follows the real shape — the glyphs, the contour, or the silhouette of an image's TRANSPARENCY: a PNG cut-out becomes a die-cut solid. Exact.
- `fit: "box"` (default for video and nested comps) is a slab the size of the picture — built once, whatever plays on it. Fast; right for photos and footage.
- Sides: `sideColor`, or automatic — a shade of a vector's face, or a picture's own edge pixels drawn back along the depth (a printed block).

Key `extrude.depth` from 0 for letters growing out of a wall; `depth: 0` makes it flat again. How light treats it is its material: a thick layer is lit by default; set `world_material {ids, preset}` (gold letters, a glass logo, a chrome badge) or `material.roughness` / `material.metalness` / `material.emission` like a mesh's. A FLAT picture stays unlit (its exact colours) until you give it a material.

## Painting a surface

People paint straight onto an object in the viewport (Paint, P): the paint lands on a drawing layer in the comp the mesh wears as its texture, which is made for it on the first stroke (a base coat in its colour). A mesh's `texture` can be any flat comp — painted with the 2D tools (pixel brushes, vectors, text, even animation) — wrapping the shape live: `layer_patch` the mesh with `texture: {frameId, repeat}` and `uv: "box" | "planar" | "cylinder" | "sphere"` (planar = the front, cylinder for cans and bottles, sphere for planets). Build the comp like any design; a label, a pattern, a looping animated screen on a phone model.

## Strokes on 3D

Give a mesh a stroke (`layer_patch` `strokes[-]`) and it outlines the shape as the camera sees it, plus its hard edges — a toon/line-art look. `lines` chooses which edges (`silhouette`, `creases` + `creaseAngle`, `boundaries`, `marked`). Width is in screen pixels.

A 2D layer standing in a world (a title, a logo, a photo card) gets the same with `world_line_art {ids, color, width, opacity?, silhouette?, creases?, creaseAngle?, boundaries?}` — its own line colour and width, because its strokes are already its 2D outline. Thick gold letters with a dark outline read like an illustration; `enabled: false` removes it. Its `lineArt.width`, `lineArt.opacity` and `lineArt.color` key like any property (`keyframes_set`) — an outline that draws on or fades.

A path with depth on its points (`z`) and a stroke is a TUBE in a world — round, lit, its width following the stroke's profile, so a pen stroke tapers as it was drawn. People also paint with pixel brushes straight onto a drawing layer standing in a world (Paint, P).

## The look — bloom and depth of field

`world_look {frameId, toneMapping?, exposure?, shadows?, mist?, bloom?, cameraId?, dof?}` is the premium-render switches in one call:

- `bloom: {enabled: true, strength 0.4–1.2, radius 0.3–0.7, threshold 0.6–0.85}` — what is brighter than the threshold glows into its surroundings. Pair with `material.emission` + `emissionStrength` 2–6 for neon, screens, sparks, a glowing logo. `source: "emission"` makes ONLY emissive materials glow — a white wall or a bright sky never blooms, a dim neon still does; the right choice for neon and screens in a lit scene.
- `dof: {focusOn: layerId | null, focusDistance?, fStop}` with `cameraId` — depth of field through that camera. `focusOn` keeps a layer sharp wherever it or the camera moves (a rack focus is two cameras cut, or `fStop` keyed); `fStop` 1.4–2.8 for a shallow product look, 8+ for nearly all sharp. Keyable at `camera.dof.fStop` / `camera.dof.focusDistance`.

- `ao: {enabled: true, radius 20–80, strength 0.6–1.2}` — contact shadows where things meet: a product sitting on a surface, letters standing on a floor.
- `floor: {mode: "shadow" | "solid" | "mirror", color?, opacity?, z?}` — `shadow` catches shadows on an invisible floor (an object grounded over a 2D layout), `mirror` reflects the scene in a glossy floor (a hero product, chrome type). Put the floor's `z` at the bottom of the objects.

All of these are on the finished picture: flat pictures keep their colours, a transparent world still glows and blurs over the 2D behind it. MOTION BLUR works in a world as in a flat comp: turn on the world comp's `motionBlur` (`frame_update`); the live camera layer's own `motionBlur` sets the shutter.

## Check your work

- `world_inspect {frameId, atMs}` — every object's world position, rotation and size, and which camera is live. Cheap: use it before rendering ("is the product in front of the camera?").
- `view_render` the world (or the flat comp that nests it). 3D renders cost more than flat ones: `glance` while blocking, `look` to judge.

## Recipes

- **Product turntable** — `starter: false`; the product at the origin with a material; environment 0.5 + sun 3; a camera at `[0, -900, 250]` with `lookAt` the product; key the product's `rotation` 0 → 360 over the shot, linear.
- **Orbit** — a camera tracking the subject (`world_track lookAt`), its `x, y` keyed around a circle (4 keys, `ease-in-out`), or put the camera in a group and key the group's `rotation`.
- **Title in space** — `world_add kind: text` at `[0, 0, 150]`, an in-animation preset (pop, slide, fade) — it plays through the camera, so a slide reads as a slide from any angle.
- **Floating 3D sticker in a 2D layout** — `world_create {transparent: true, starter: false}`, the object + environment + a camera, then `layer_create kind: comp` into the layout.
- **Photo that curls in** — the image in a world with `world_extrude {fit: "box", depth: 4}`, a `bend` modifier (`axis: "x"`) keyed `angle` 140 → 0 over 0.8 s with `ease-out`, a camera at a slight angle, `world_look bloom` off, `dof` focused on it at f/2.
- **Neon logo** — the logo's paths in a world, `world_extrude depth 12, bevel 2`, `world_material neon`, `emissionStrength` 4, a dark world, `world_look {bloom: {enabled: true, strength: 0.9}}`.
- **Shatter** — any solid with an `explode` modifier, `progress` keyed 0 → 1, `gravity` 400, `spin` 360, `stagger` 0.4, on a beat.
- **Squash and stretch on a title** — thick text, `world_lattice`, then key the cage: `mesh_edit` `scale` its `side: "+z"` row down on the landing and back over 300 ms (or key the Lattice card's `strength` with a cage already squashed).
- **Text on a ribbon** — a path with depth (`z` on its points) in the world, a text with a `curve` modifier reading it, `offset` keyed −1 → 1 so the words travel along it.
- **Product on a mirror** — `world_look {floor: {mode: "mirror", z: <bottom>}, ao: {enabled: true}, bloom: {enabled: true, strength: 0.4}}`, a dark environment, a slow turntable.

## Gotchas

- Positions are world units, not pixels-from-centre: a world's `y` is depth. Lift things with `z`.
- An object outside every camera's view renders nothing — `world_inspect` first.
- Imported models (`.glb` best) are kept as their file; `material.source: "file"` keeps the file's materials, `"custom"` uses the layer's fill and material.
- Effects (glow, blur, shadow, grade) work on a single mesh too (`layer_patch` its `effects` like any layer) — the object is rendered alone, still hidden by what stands in front of it. A 2D layer's effects are part of its picture (they ride on its face). For real bloom and lens blur use `world_look`; for a CAMERA FILTER (a grade on what the camera sees), put an adjustment layer with reach `all` in the world itself; cut it with the camera clips to change the look per shot.
- Thickness on an OPEN path (a stroke with no closed contour) has nothing to extrude — it stays a line. Close it, or use `fit: "box"`.
- People export worlds as glTF/GLB (animated), USDZ, OBJ and STL from the Export panel or a right-click on objects; a project's worlds save inside the .motion file like everything else.

## Organs, merged solids, and components in a world

- **Build a face from organs**: `world_add {kind:"head", eyes:false}` is a
  head with sockets; eyeballs are spheres in them; lids are shells —
  `uvSphere {arc: 90}` is a cap open at its rim — posed by a pose set;
  `world_add {scale}` sizes anything.
- **`union` keeps each member's colour** and outlines the merged shape once
  (silhouette and the creases where parts meet — never an eye's rim inside the
  head). Put closed solids in a union; open shells (lids) outside it, where
  they draw their own lid line.
- **A component made in a world is a world**, content in place, with copies of
  the camera and lights for its own picture. Placed in a world, an instance is
  its 3D objects (not a flat picture), lit and seen by the world it stands in.
